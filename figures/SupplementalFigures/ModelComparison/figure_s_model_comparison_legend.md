**Supplemental Figure S_x | Head-to-head comparison of NMD-prediction models on long-read-observed isoforms, benchmarked against the mashr posterior-mean log fold-change under SMG1 inhibition.** Two variant-level NMD prediction models from the published literature — NMDetective-B (Lindeboom *et al.* 2019, decision-tree with thresholds 50 / 150 / 407 nt) and the NMDEP-re-thresholded rule baseline (Saadat & Fellay 2025, same decision-tree with thresholds 49 / 120 / 355 nt) — are scored against our model (this work) on the same long-read isoforms. Scoring is restricted to the 561-isoform intersection on which all three models produce predictions (NMD+/PTC+ = 255, NMD+/PTC− = 30, Control = 276); our model's predictions are available only on the held-out test subset of the n = 1,166 ref-AUG-traceable cohort. Continuous gold standard for each isoform is the mashr posterior-mean log₂ fold-change of its parent transcript under SMG1i (4-CT cross-cell-type estimate). **(A)** Predicted NMD score (x-axis; rule-based score for NMDetective-B and NMDEP, predicted NMD probability for our model) vs gold-standard log fold-change (y-axis). Points are coloured by PTC subclass. Pooled Spearman correlation is annotated within each subplot. All three models show similar pooled-level correlation (≈0.76–0.77), driven primarily by the easy NMD-positive vs Control separation. **(B)** Within-subclass Spearman correlation per model: scoring each model only on the isoforms within a single PTC subclass removes the easy between-class separation and isolates each model's ability to discriminate NMD-substrate-level variation among isoforms in the same subclass. Within the NMD+/PTC+ subclass (n = 255), our model achieves Spearman 0.34 vs 0.19–0.22 for the rule-based models. Within the NMD+/PTC− subclass (n = 30; PTC-negative substrates), the rule-based models are anti-correlated with truth (Spearman ≈ −0.14) by construction — these isoforms lack a TD2-detectable PTC and so receive low NMDetective/NMDEP scores despite being real NMD substrates by the mashr gold standard — whereas our model attains Spearman 0.51. Within the Control subclass (n = 276), rule-based models have essentially no signal (≈0.01); our model retains Spearman 0.23.
