**Supplemental Figure S_x | Head-to-head comparison of NMD-prediction models on long-read-observed isoforms, benchmarked against the mashr posterior-mean log fold-change under SMG1 inhibition.** Two variant-level NMD prediction models from the published literature — NMDetective-B (Lindeboom *et al.* 2019, decision-tree with thresholds 50 / 150 / 407 nt) and the NMDEP-re-thresholded rule baseline (Saadat & Fellay 2025, same decision-tree with thresholds 49 / 120 / 355 nt) — are scored against our model (this work) on the same long-read isoforms. Scoring is restricted to the 2,218-isoform intersection on which all three models produce predictions (NMD+/PTC+ = 1,016, NMD+/PTC− = 95, Control = 1,107); our model's predictions span its full H5 universe (train + val + test splits combined). Continuous gold standard for each isoform is the mashr posterior-mean log₂ fold-change of its parent transcript under SMG1i (4-CT cross-cell-type estimate). **(A)** Predicted NMD score (x-axis; rule-based score for NMDetective-B and NMDEP, predicted NMD probability for our model) vs gold-standard log fold-change (y-axis). Points are coloured by PTC subclass. Pooled Spearman correlation is annotated within each subplot. All three models show similar pooled-level correlation (≈0.77–0.79), driven primarily by the easy NMD-positive vs Control separation. **(B)** Within-subclass Spearman correlation per model: scoring each model only on the isoforms within a single PTC subclass removes the easy between-class separation and isolates each model's ability to discriminate NMD-substrate-level variation among isoforms in the same subclass. Within the NMD+/PTC+ subclass (n = 1,016), our model achieves Spearman 0.32 vs 0.16 for both rule-based models. Within the NMD+/PTC− subclass (n = 95; PTC-negative substrates), the rule-based models are anti-correlated with truth (Spearman −0.17) by construction — these isoforms lack a TD2-detectable PTC and so receive low NMDetective/NMDEP scores despite being real NMD substrates by the mashr gold standard — whereas our model attains Spearman 0.43. Within the Control subclass (n = 1,107), rule-based models have essentially no signal (≈0.06); our model retains Spearman 0.19. **Test-only sensitivity:** because our model's full-cohort predictions include training-set isoforms, we re-computed the within-subclass head-to-head restricted to the chr1/3/5/7 held-out test split (NMD+/PTC+ = 255, NMD+/PTC− = 30, Control = 276). Within-subclass Spearmans were directionally identical and quantitatively similar (0.34 / 0.51 / 0.23 for our model; 0.19 / −0.14 / 0.01 for NMDetective-B), confirming that the qualitative pattern is not a train-set inflation artifact.
